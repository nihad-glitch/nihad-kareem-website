The Ongoing Conflict in the Middle East and Its Impact on Iraq and Kurdistan

By Nihad Aras Kareem

The Middle East continues to experience one of the most unstable and dangerous periods in recent history. Iraq, Iran, Israel, the United States, and the Gulf countries remain deeply involved in political and military tensions that threaten the future stability of the region. The wars in Gaza, Syria, and other parts of the Middle East have increased fears that the conflict could continue for many years and possibly expand into a larger regional war.

Iraq still suffers from corruption, political division, economic weakness, and the growing influence of armed militias. Although Iraq is one of the richest oil-producing countries in the world, millions of Iraqi citizens continue to face poverty, unemployment, poor electricity, and weak public services. Corruption inside the Iraqi political system remains one of the biggest obstacles preventing development and stability. Many Iraqi citizens believe that political parties and armed groups care more about power and money than improving the lives of ordinary people.

The Kurdistan Region also faces serious internal problems, including corruption, political rivalry between parties, economic difficulties, and delayed salaries for public workers. Many young people in both Iraq and the Kurdistan Region have lost trust in political leaders because they believe corruption has weakened state institutions and damaged the economy. Public frustration continues to grow because of unemployment, lack of opportunities, and political instability.

Iran remains one of the most powerful actors in Iraq and the wider Middle East. Tehran supports several Shiite militias inside Iraq that are politically and militarily influential. Some of these militias operate outside the full control of the Iraqi government and continue to launch attacks against American interests and sometimes against the Kurdistan Region. Iran argues that these groups are necessary to resist foreign influence and protect regional security, but critics believe they increase instability and weaken Iraqi sovereignty.

The Kurdistan Region has become increasingly vulnerable to attacks by Iran-backed militias and Iranian missile strikes. Erbil and other Kurdish areas have been targeted by rockets and drones several times in recent years. Militias accuse the Kurdistan Region of cooperating with the United States and Israel, accusations strongly rejected by Kurdish officials. These attacks have created fear among civilians and damaged investor confidence in the region.

At the same time, tensions between Baghdad and Erbil continue over oil exports, budget disputes, territorial disagreements, and political authority. Although both sides try to avoid direct confrontation, mistrust remains strong. The continued presence of militias inside Iraq creates fears that internal conflict could return at any moment, especially if regional tensions between Iran, Israel, and the United States continue to rise.

Many political analysts believe that war and instability may continue in the Middle East for a long time because the root causes of the conflicts have not been solved. Foreign interference, sectarian divisions, armed militias, economic inequality, and weak governments continue to fuel violence across the region. The rivalry between Iran and Israel has become one of the most dangerous conflicts in the Middle East, and Iraq often becomes a battleground for this competition.

The continuation of war would have serious consequences for Iraq and the Kurdistan Region. More conflict could damage the economy, reduce foreign investment, increase migration, and create further insecurity for civilians. Young people may continue leaving the country in search of better opportunities abroad. If reforms are not implemented and regional tensions are not reduced through diplomacy, the future of Iraq and the Kurdistan Region may remain uncertain for many years.

In conclusion, Iraq and the Kurdistan Region face major challenges caused by corruption, political instability, militia influence, and regional conflict. The growing tensions involving Iran, Israel, and the United States increase the risk of continued war and instability throughout the Middle East. Without strong reforms, national unity, and serious diplomatic efforts, peace in the region may remain difficult to achieve.
